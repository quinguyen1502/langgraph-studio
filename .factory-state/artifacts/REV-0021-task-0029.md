## How the rules were applied
- Layering: models (`team.py`, `audit_event.py`) → repositories (`teams`, `audit`, `user_data`, `workflows.set_owner/referencing`) → services (`team`, `ownership`, `audit`, `policy`, plus share/user/workflow/run/channel/mcp/approval) → thin routes. Collaborators are optional constructor arguments, so fakes keep working.
- DRY: user deletion reuses `OwnershipService.reassign`; the effective share role goes through `permissions.best_share` inside `AccessService` (the single check); audit entries are written only by the service that made the change (and only when something actually changed).
- KISS: flat teams, one policy key, the children-changed note is computed when read (nothing stored).
- Envelope + English codes: 11 new codes (catalog, docs/api-errors.md, en/vi).
- Migration `0022_teams_audit`: SQLite + Postgres, tested on both.
- Strict shadcn: Card/Table/Select/Switch/Dialog/Alert/Badge, tokens only.

## Security
- Teams, the audit log, the policy and user deletion are admin-only (`users.manage`). Transfer needs the `transfer` action (owner/admin); the target must be an active editor/admin.
- References the new owner cannot use are never silently kept: they are re-bound to the target's own resources (validated: `TRANSFER_REBIND_INVALID`) or flagged.
- The org-resource policy is checked against the run's actor at run time (MCP call, approval channel), not only in the UI.
- Deletion: self/last-admin guards, owned workflows must be reassigned first, personal secrets are purged, and the anonymized tombstone carries no e-mail.

## Remaining / follow-ups
- No audit retention or export; no nested teams or SSO group sync.
- Flagged references after a transfer must be fixed by hand.
- Slack Enterprise Grid out of scope (DEC-0024).
